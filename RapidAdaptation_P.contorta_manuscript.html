Survival

Of the originally planted trees, only 56.6% of the Origin trees survived, while 67.7% of the Plantation and 73% of the Regeneration trees survived (Figure 2).Survival differed significantly among generations (binomial GLM, likelihood ratio test: X^2 = 18.2, p < 0.001) Estimated marginal mean survival probabilities varied among generations. Survival was significantly lower in Origin trees than in Origin trees (p < 0.001) and to a lesser extent Plantation trees (p = 0.04), whereas the difference between Plantation and Regeneration was not significant (p = 0.4).

Figure 2 Survival (%) of trees in each cohort. Bars represent the proportion of trees at the end of growing season three recorded as alive.

Influence of cohort on trait values

If traits had a correlation index greater than 0.6, the more unique and biologically significant trait was retained for subsequent analyses. As RMF and shoot-to-root ratio are the inverse of each other, only RMF was kept as it gives more insight into below-ground allocation.
